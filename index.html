Do I need it?

A quick guide to whether the CRA covers your software.

This checklist generally applies when you supply software on the EU market as part of a commercial activity and its intended or reasonably foreseeable use involves exchanging data with another device or network. It can be paid or free.

Cloud services and websites. Standalone cloud services, ordinary websites and browser-only SaaS are generally outside scope. Software supplied for local execution can still be covered, even without a traditional installation.

Cloud backends. A backend can be part of a covered product when it is developed by or under the responsibility of its manufacturer and the product cannot perform one of its functions without it.

Exclusions. Free and open-source software supplied outside a commercial activity and certain products covered by other sector rules are excluded. Some open-source stewards have separate, limited duties.

ExampleUsual position
Desktop or mobile appCovered, subject to the conditions above.
Browser extension, portable executable, server software or separately supplied libraryCan be covered, even without a traditional installation process.
Ordinary website or browser-only SaaSGenerally outside scope.
Installed app that depends on a cloud backendThe app can be covered, together with the qualifying backend functionality.
Firmware or connected hardwareAlso covered; CRA extends beyond apps.

Use this as a first check. Confirm the product classification and any exclusions before relying on the checklist.

A written security review

A document explains the product’s security risks, how they are addressed and why any security requirement does not apply. It is updated when risks or the product change.

02

A product that meets its security requirements

Evidence covers safe defaults, access controls, data protection, resilience, logging and secure data deletion, plus all other applicable Annex I requirements. Security testing happens regularly. When placed on the market, the product has no known security flaws that attackers can exploit.

03

A checked inventory of software components

A machine-readable SBOM lists at least the libraries the product directly depends on. Third-party components are checked for security. Newly discovered component vulnerabilities and relevant fixes are shared with their maintainers.

SBOM = Software Bill of Materials

A software ingredients list: components, their versions and dependencies. Keep it machine-readable, for example in SPDX or CycloneDX format, and cover at least the dependencies your product uses directly.

04

A working process for handling vulnerabilities

Users can find a security-reporting contact and disclosure policy. Reports are recorded and assessed, and vulnerabilities are addressed promptly according to risk. Published security notices explain fixed issues, affected versions, impact, severity and what users should do. A justified publication delay is allowed where necessary to protect users.

A reporting contact is required; a dedicated security email is optional

Provide an easily identifiable contact for direct, rapid vulnerability reporting. Allow a choice of communication methods and do not restrict contact to automated tools. A monitored security mailbox is a practical option, not a specifically prescribed address or format.

Article 13(17); Annex I, Part II, point 6; Annex II, point 2 ↗

05

A secure way to deliver security updates

Updates reach users securely and promptly, with clear instructions. They are free unless a permitted agreement for a tailor-made business product says otherwise. Security fixes are separate from feature updates where technically feasible. Where automatic updates apply, they are on by default, with options to disable or postpone them.

06

A clear support commitment

The support period matches how long the product is expected to be used—at least five years unless expected use is shorter. Buyers can see the support end month/year. Each issued security update remains available for at least ten years after release, or the remaining support period if longer.

Make the security-support end date clear at purchase

Users must be able to see the support end month and year at purchase. The duration must meet CRA rules for expected use. A subscription matters where it genuinely limits how long the software remains usable; it does not automatically shorten support. Covered free products follow the same rules.

Article 13(8), (19); recital 60 ↗

07

A working process for reporting serious security events

Already required · since 11 September 2026

The company can report actively exploited vulnerabilities and qualifying severe incidents to ENISA and the relevant national CSIRT without undue delay: initial warning within 24 hours, details within 72 hours. Final reports follow within 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure becomes available for vulnerabilities, or one month after the incident notification. Affected users are informed.

Reporting readiness is required now

These reporting duties have applied since 11 September 2026, including to covered products placed on the market before 11 December 2027. The process must already be ready to use when a reportable event occurs.

Articles 14, 69(3) and 71 ↗

08

Clear security information for users

Users receive product and manufacturer identification, contact details, support information and access to the conformity declaration. Instructions explain safe setup, security risks, updates, secure removal from service and data deletion. The language is understandable to the intended users.

Users need understandable security instructions

English is sufficient only where it meets the needs of users and market surveillance authorities and applicable language rules. Provide required translations. The EU Declaration of Conformity has a separate rule: provide it in the languages required by each EU country where the product is marketed.

09

Completed compliance assessment and records

The required assessment is completed, the EU Declaration of Conformity is signed and CE marking is applied correctly. Technical records contain the design, security review, test results and vulnerability-handling details. Records, declaration and required user instructions are maintained and available for at least ten years after market placement, or the support period if longer.

CE marking also applies to software

For software covered by the CRA, place the CE mark on the EU Declaration of Conformity or the accompanying website. The website section must be easily and directly accessible to consumers.

10

A way to handle changes and unsafe products

Product changes are checked for their effect on compliance, with reassessment when required. If the product becomes non-compliant, the company takes corrective action, withdraws or recalls it where necessary, and cooperates with authorities. Required notices also cover business closure that prevents continued compliance.

If the business closes

If closure prevents continued CRA compliance, notify the relevant market surveillance authorities before closing. Inform affected users by available means, as far as possible. Keep copies of the notices as evidence.

2. Important exceptions

Use an exception only when the supporting evidence justifies it.

Where applicable

Justify each inapplicable security requirement in the product risk assessment; retain the product context, reasoning and reviewer approval. Do not treat an unanswered item as an exception.

Article 13(2)–(3); Annex I, Part I

Technically feasible

For combined security and feature updates, retain an engineering explanation and supporting tests showing why separation is not feasible.

Annex I, Part II, point 2

Expected product lifetime

Support may be shorter than five years only when expected use is shorter. Retain a justified lifetime assessment, user expectations and the published end date.

Article 13(8)–(9), (19)

Tailor-made business agreements

Retain the applicable business-user agreement and evidence that the product is tailor-made before relying on the permitted exceptions for update charges or default configuration.

Annex I, Part I, point 2(b); Part II, point 8

Delayed security notices

Retain the documented user-protection reason, disclosure decision and review date for any delay in publishing details of a fixed vulnerability.

Annex I, Part II, point 4

Automatic updates

Assess applicability using the product risk assessment. Recital 56 explains component-integration and professional-environment cases. Retain the intended-use justification and update tests. Where automatic updates apply, verify default enablement, opt-out, postponement and user notifications.

Annex I, Part I, point 2(c); Part II, point 7; recital 56

Reporting thresholds and clocks

Record awareness time and the Article 14 reporting trigger. For severe incidents, the final-report month runs from the incident notification; for exploited vulnerabilities, the 14-day period runs from availability of a corrective or mitigating measure.

Article 14(2), (4)–(5)
Check scope and transition first.

Main obligations apply from 11 December 2027. Article 14 reporting obligations have applied since 11 September 2026. Check product scope and transitional rules in Articles 2 and 69.
